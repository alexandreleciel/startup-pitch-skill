# Question bank: investor questions by theme

What this file is for: the themed bank of questions investors ask, with the intent behind the ones that most often end a process.
Read it as a lookup when `investor-questions.md` says to fill a theme for the stage and investor type. Look up, do not read through.
Questions derived from the startup's own weak spots come first (`investor-questions.md` section 1, or `core.md` in a quick run); this bank only fills the themes they leave uncovered.

## Contents (look up the theme you need)

Pick from these; do not paste the whole bank into `investor-qa.md`. Questions marked with "Intent:" are the
ones that most often end a process; the intent line is what the answer must actually address (the
surface question / intent / universal probe pattern comes from lowesyang's DD patterns). Phrase them
in the investor's register, not the founder's.

## 1. Market and why now
1. What problem are you solving, in two sentences, and for whom exactly?
2. How did you size the market? Walk me through the bottom-up.
   Intent: is there a bottom-up at all, or a top-down report times a percentage?
3. Why has nobody done this already? Who tried and failed, and why?
   Intent: either you know the graveyard or you are about to join it.
4. Why now? What changed in technology, regulation, cost or behaviour in the last two years?
5. What are customers doing today without you, and what does that cost them?
6. Is this a painkiller or a vitamin? How do you know?
7. How fast is the market growing, and what is your own thesis about how it changes?
8. What realistic share can you capture, and over what period?

## 2. Product and insight
1. What is your unique insight, and how did you earn it?
   Intent: insight from customers and personal experience, not from a trend piece.
2. Show me the product. What does the user do in the first five minutes?
3. What did early versions teach you that changed the product?
4. Which decisions are made by the system and which by humans? Explain a failure mode in plain language.
   Intent (AI and algorithmic products): do you understand your own system, or is it a black box to you too?
5. What would take a well-funded competitor more than 12 months to replicate, and why?
6. What is the next thing you build, and what metric moves because of it?
7. What happens when the product is wrong, down or attacked?
8. Have you watched a user struggle with it without helping? What surprised you?

## 3. Traction and proof
1. What is your progress relative to the time and money spent?
2. What is your growth from what base, over what period, month by month?
   Intent: the rate hides the base; the monthly series shows the shape.
3. Show me retention by cohort. Does the curve flatten or keep falling?
   Intent: is growth durable, or are new users backfilling churn (a leaky bucket)?
4. Which of these numbers are organic and which are paid or incentivised?
5. Who is your best customer, by name, and why did they buy?
6. What happens if your largest customer leaves?
7. How many of these users are paying, and how many are pilots, trials or free?
8. Reconcile your top metric from two sources (billing and CRM, or product and payments).
   Intent: data integrity; the single most common DD trip-wire across stages.
9. If you have no users, what is the strongest evidence of demand that is behaviour, not interest?

## 4. Business model and unit economics
1. How do you make money, in one sentence?
2. What are CAC, gross margin and payback, and how are they trending?
3. What is fixed and what is variable in your costs? When revenue grows 10x, do costs grow 2x or 10x?
   Intent: is there operating leverage, or does this scale like a services business?
4. Who pays, who uses, and who signs? Are they the same person?
5. How did you set the price, and what happened when you raised it?
6. Which revenue is recurring, which is one-off, and which is still a letter of intent?
7. When are you default alive (profitable before the cash runs out) on current burn?
8. What is your burn, your runway, and the month you would need to start raising again?
9. If you stopped all paid marketing tomorrow, what would growth be?

## 5. Competition and moat
1. Who are your competitors, including the spreadsheet and the status quo?
2. Pick one rival. Why do they matter and how do you win against them?
3. Why will the incumbent not build this?
   Intent: a founder who refuses to concede that they could blows the answer; concede, then explain why it is cheaper to buy or ignore you.
4. What stops a fast follower with more money?
5. What is the moat in real vocabulary: switching costs, network effects, proprietary data, standards, scale economies, counter-positioning?
   Intent: "relationships", "brand", "culture" and "first mover" are the shallow answers that get passes.
6. Where are competitors genuinely stronger than you? What is your win rate head to head, and why do you lose?
7. If a large platform launched this as a feature tomorrow, what happens to you?
8. Who are the three to five most likely acquirers, and why would they pay a premium?

## 6. Team and hiring
1. Who are you, in one line each, with the proof relevant to this company?
2. How long have you known each other, and how was the trust earned?
3. Why this problem? What scar tissue led you here?
4. Is everyone full-time? What is the equity split?
   Intent: commitment and fairness; an uneven split without a story is a flag.
5. Who writes the code? Who sells?
6. What is the biggest hole in the team, and who fills it with this money?
   Intent: a founder who names the hole is trusted more than one who hypes a hire.
7. What was your worst disagreement as founders, and how did it end?
8. Do you know the next ten people you need, by name? What is each founder's personal runway?

## 7. Go-to-market
1. How will you get the first 1,000 customers (or the first ten enterprise accounts)?
2. Which single channel works repeatably today, and what does it cost per customer?
3. Where do new users come from, and what makes them try you?
4. How long is the sales cycle, who is the champion, who blocks?
5. What is the land-and-expand path inside one customer?
6. How do you expand from today's segment to the one you need in three years? What is the bridge?
   Intent: beachhead or niche you are stuck in?
7. What is the biggest growth bottleneck right now, and what is the backup if the main channel breaks?
8. What would it take to reach a specific revenue milestone, in hires and months?

## 8. Financials and use of funds
1. How much are you raising and what does it buy, milestone by milestone?
2. What is the burn after the raise, and how many months does that give you?
3. What are the three assumptions your model is most sensitive to?
   Intent: does the founder know the levers, or just the output?
4. What did you learn from missing (or beating) your last plan?
5. What if you raise half? What if you raise double? How much goes to founder salaries?
6. What milestone makes the next round easy, and when do you hit it?
7. Which numbers in your plan are facts, which are assumptions, which are projections?
8. Are you sharing the same forecast your board (or existing investors) sees?

## 9. The round itself
1. What valuation are you asking, and how did you arrive at it?
   Intent: price discipline; never negotiate against yourself (`investor-questions.md` section 3).
2. Who else is in the process, and who has committed?
   Intent: momentum and social proof; naming firms backfires either way.
3. Are existing investors participating? With how much?
4. Why our firm? Why me?
5. What is your timeline to close?
6. What instrument: priced round, SAFE, note? Why? How much is allocated and how much is left for a lead?
7. What is on the cap table today: SAFEs, notes, option pool, advisors?
8. Why raise now rather than in six months with better numbers?

## 10. Risks and what kills you
1. What is the biggest risk to the business, and what are you doing about it?
   Intent: self-awareness; "nothing" or "raising money" fails.
2. What is the worst-case scenario, and how do you detect it early?
3. What part of the plan are you least confident about?
4. What would make you shut this down?
5. What is something true about this market that most people disagree with?
6. What keeps you up at night?
7. What is the single point of failure: a person, a supplier, a platform, a customer?
8. What happens in the worst 10 percent of conditions (downturn, model degradation, supply shock)?
9. If this fails in two years, what is the most likely cause?

## 11. Diligence and legal
1. Is the company incorporated, where, and is the cap table clean?
2. Has every founder, employee and contractor signed an IP assignment?
   Intent: the classic landmine; one missing signature can stall a close.
3. Was any core IP developed at a previous employer, a university or under a grant?
4. Any copyleft open-source dependencies (GPL, AGPL) in the commercial product?
5. Patents filed, granted or pending? Any freedom-to-operate analysis?
6. Any pending or threatened litigation?
7. Data privacy posture (GDPR, CCPA, or equivalents): what data, where stored, what consent?
8. Licences, certifications or regulatory status required in your domain, and where are you on them?
9. Who holds custody of customer funds or assets, and what happens to them if you fail?
10. Can I speak to three customers, including one who churned?
