# Stage benchmarks, with provenance and vintage

What this file is for: the round sizes, valuations, traction bars and timing figures quoted for pre-seed, seed and Series A, each with its source, its year and whether it was re-checked against the primary page.
Read it only when an output will quote a stage benchmark, or a founder file quotes one that needs checking. Quote with source and year, or not at all.
Several figures that other skills attribute to CRV 2026 are not on CRV's live page (checked 2026-11-10); sections 2 and 4 say which ones, and what to quote instead.

## 1. Pre-seed

- Mean round about 1.52M USD (FundraiseInsider, early 2025); around 42 percent of Carta pre-seed rounds under 250K USD (Carta, Q3 2024); the 1M to 2.5M band shrank from 24 percent (Q1 2023, historical) to 18 percent (Carta, Q1 2026). Source chain: pedronauck stage-benchmarks.md; not re-verified.
- Instruments: SAFEs about 90 percent of rounds; convertible notes 7 percent (Carta, Q1 2026, via pedronauck).
- Traction expectation: DocSend's pre-seed research reports that 89 to 90 percent of pre-seed companies raising had a product at least in alpha (DocSend, 2020, historical; re-checked on the Dropbox DocSend research page). The often-quoted "only about 11 percent were pure idea stage" is the inverse of that figure, not a separately published statistic; say "about nine in ten had a product in alpha or later" instead.
- Reader attention: investors spent over 4 minutes on funded pre-seed decks and about 1.5 minutes on unfunded ones (DocSend, 2020, historical); seed decks get about 3 minutes 44 seconds and 58 percent of decks are read to the last slide (DocSend, cited by CRV, 2026).
- Graduation pre-seed to seed about 50 percent (Incisive, 2025, via pedronauck; not re-verified).

## 2. Seed

- Round size 2.5M to 5M USD, hot AI and deep tech 6M to 10M (startups.com and Carta, 2025, via pedronauck); median deal 3.1M (PitchBook/NVCA, 2024, via pedronauck).
- Valuation, three populations that must not be mixed: median 12.8M post (Carta 2024, all seed rounds); 20M to 30M, median about 24M (startups.com 2025, priced seed only); AI seed median pre-money 17.9M, about 42 percent above non-AI (eqvista, 2026). All via pedronauck.
- Dilution: 20.1 percent actual median (Carta, 2024, via pedronauck).
- ARR bar, conflict stated: the live CRV guide says companies "often need between 250,000 and 1 million USD in ARR for an institutional seed round" (CRV, "Pitching to Investors", dated July 2026; fetched 2026-11-10). pedronauck stage-benchmarks.md attributes "500K to 1.5M ARR for a strong B2B SaaS seed" to CRV; that range does not appear on the live page. Quote the live CRV range and treat the 500K to 1.5M range as unverified secondary.
- Growth, churn, NRR and payback bars at seed: pedronauck attributes "15 to 20 percent MoM strong, over 25 percent exceptional", "monthly churn under 2.5 to 5 percent", "NRR at or above 100 percent" and "CAC payback under 12 months" to CRV 2026. None of these strings appear on the live CRV page (checked 2026-11-10). Do not cite them as CRV. Usable alternatives: a16z's "16 Startup Metrics" names no bar; YC's "10 percent per week sustained is interesting" is YC's long-standing framing (historical, via pedronauck). When no sourced bar exists, describe the principle (growth that compounds for 6 or more months, gross churn low enough that the base retains) rather than a threshold.
- Process: the CRV guide says to start at least 6 months before the zero-cash date and budget at least 3 months for the active process (CRV, 2026); the "40 to 60 meetings" and "a raise dragging past 4 months is a negative signal" figures attributed to CRV in pedronauck are not on the live page and should not be quoted as CRV. Carta's median seed close of 142 days in 2025 vs 68 in 2021 (via pedronauck; not re-verified) is the only timing statistic with a named population.

## 3. Seed to Series A conversion

- Conversion 30 to 35 percent, down from about 50 percent pre-2022 (startups.com/Carta, 2025, via pedronauck). UK: 12 percent of seeded companies reach A (Antler, via pedronauck; vintage not stated, treat as unverified).
- Timing (Carta, 3,365 US startups raising a Series A between Q1 2018 and Q3 2025; summarised by Jason Lemkin, SaaStr, October 2025; verified 2026-11-10): 39 percent of Q3 2025 Series A raisers took 3 or more years from seed, vs 19 percent in Q3 2019; only 23 percent raised within 1.5 to 2 years, vs 41 percent in 2019; about 15 percent within 12 months (Peter Walker, Carta, quoted in the same piece). CRV's guide states a median gap of about 20 months and advises planning on a 2-year horizon (CRV citing Carta Q2 2025).
- Consequence for the use-of-funds slide: Lemkin's advice is to plan the seed for about 3 years at 75K to 100K USD monthly burn rather than 200K (SaaStr, 2025). This is advice, not a benchmark.
- What the seed must buy, one source's worked path for a SaaS company over about 20 months: ARR 200K to 1.5M or 2M, net new ARR 20K to 80K or 120K per month, team 4 to 12 or 18, NDR 105 to 120 percent, gross margin 70 to 80 percent, logo retention above 85 percent, burn multiple 1.5x to 2.5x (startups.com, 2025, via pedronauck; one advisory source, not a population statistic).

## 4. Series A

- ARR bar is a range and a conflict, not a number. Live CRV guide: "current expectations are often closer to 1 to 5 million USD in ARR, depending on the category" in the body, and "closer to 5 million to 10 million" in its FAQ, with AI-first products possibly lower (CRV, 2026; both quotes fetched 2026-11-10). Other advisory bars via pedronauck: 1M to 2M "clears the door" (startups.com, 2025); 1.5M to 3M (SaaS CFO, October 2025); "3M is the new 1M" (Graham and Walker, 2025). The "2M to 5M wins a competitive process" line pedronauck attributes to CRV is not on the live page.
- Actual distribution, which the advice ignores: 35.6 percent of Series A rounds had zero revenue and 69.8 percent were under 1M ARR (Carta, 2024, via pedronauck; not re-verified). The gap between advice and actuals is survivorship of competitive processes plus non-SaaS models. Tell the founder both.
- Unit economics investors commonly examine: CAC payback of 12 to 18 months and LTV:CAC of at least three to one; "healthy" SaaS gross margin with no percentage given (CRV, 2026, verified). Growth of 2x to 3x year over year as top tier, and the bar on the way to 10M ARR moving from 3x to 5x or more (Lemkin, October 2025, via pedronauck). NRR 110 to 120 percent as pricing power, GRR as the health metric, logo retention above 85 percent, magic number above 1.0 (pedronauck stage-benchmarks.md synthesis, 2025 to 2026, sources not individually named; present as practitioner heuristics).
- Round: 5M to 15M USD (Carta via CRV, per pedronauck); median valuation 41.5M (Carta, 2024); dilution median 17.9 percent (Carta, Q1 2025); AI premium about 38 percent (Carta, 2025). All via pedronauck.
- Burn multiple context: Sacks' original piece calls about 2x reasonable for early stage and 3x or more a possible product-market-fit problem, with seed companies often near 3 and falling toward 2 after the A (Sacks, 2020, historical).
- Gross margin reference for software generally: 80 to 90 percent (a16z, 2015, historical); AI products see `metrics-by-model.md` section 6.

## 5. How to use a benchmark in the pitch

Write it as context with its population: "Carta's 2024 Series A median valuation was 41.5M USD; we are raising at X because Y." Never write "the Series A bar is 2M ARR". When two sources conflict, show the range and name both. Put the citation in the slide footer or the appendix, and in `gaps.md` list any benchmark the founder quoted that you could not source.
