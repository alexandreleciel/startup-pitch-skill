# Founder perspective (Priya), October 2026

Written for the Series A narrative. Candid version. Grace and Hannah will sanitise.

## What we actually are

We are a **demand aggregator for physiotherapy** that sells to employers. Clinics have empty slots on weekday
mornings and afternoons. Employers have people in pain who wait 14 weeks for the NHS or 12 days for the insurer.
We connect the two in 2 days and take 22%. Everything else (triage, exercise programmes, outcomes dashboards)
exists to make the employer renew and the clinic prioritise us.

## What I believe

1. **In-person is not going away.** The US digital MSK players raised billions on the idea that an app and a
   sensor replace a physiotherapist. For maybe half of cases, fine. For the warehouse worker with an acute lower
   back, no. Our data: 40% of cases get hands-on treatment, and those are the expensive absences.
2. **Employers, not insurers, are the right buyer in the UK.** Insurers optimise for claims cost. Employers
   optimise for people being at work. Those are different products.
3. **The clinic network is the moat, but only if we are their best channel.** We are not exclusive and will never
   be. We win by filling slots they could not fill otherwise and paying in 14 days. The day a clinic prefers a
   competitor's referrals we are in trouble.
4. **Outcomes data is the thing that gets us from "nice benefit" to "line item".** The Brightmoor result (34%
   fewer sick days) is the most powerful sentence we have. It is also from one employer with no control group,
   and I do not want us to become the company that overclaimed. Controlled study in 2027, non-negotiable.

## The churn problem, honestly

We have lost 7 employers in 12 months. All seven had under 200 employees. The pattern: an HR manager loves us,
buys us, the CFO looks at the invoice 9 months later, utilisation is 6 people, cancels. Our product is built for
employers where someone owns "absence" as a metric. Under ~250 employees nobody does.

Decision: we stop selling under 250 from Q4 2026. Our SMB logo churn (~22% annualised) will look ugly on a
slide for another 2 quarters. Enterprise logo churn is 0 so far (small n, 2 years).

## Why now

- NHS MSK waiting lists have more than doubled since 2020 and will not recover this decade.
- Employers have a labour shortage in exactly the roles that get MSK injuries (logistics, care, manufacturing,
  retail). Absence is now a hiring problem.
- The occupational health market is finally being disrupted; the incumbents are slow and paper-based.
- Physiotherapy private practice is fragmented (thousands of small clinics) and under-utilised, with no
  aggregator.

## What I want investors to take away

Kestrel is the fastest way to get an employee in pain in front of a physiotherapist in the UK, with the
outcomes data to prove it mattered. We are at £1.9M quarterly GMV, growing 30%+ QoQ, and the enterprise cohort
is retaining at 118%. The next £9.5M gets us to a £10M GMV run-rate and the first European market.
